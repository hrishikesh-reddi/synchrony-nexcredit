Hello, I am Hrishikesh Reddy Gavinolla from Mahindra University, and this is NexCredit AI, my solution for Synchrony Hackathon Problem Statement Three: building a real-time, multi-modal underwriting engine for New-to-Credit and thin-file customers.

Traditional credit models depend heavily on bureau scores and long financial histories. That creates a structural disadvantage for potentially creditworthy gig workers, students, self-employed applicants, and first-time borrowers who may not have an established credit file. NexCredit AI changes the starting question from “Does this person already have a credit score?” to “What permitted evidence can help an underwriter understand this applicant responsibly?”

The product is a governed underwriting workbench rather than an autonomous loan-approval bot. It combines alternative application signals, document evidence, fraud-risk indicators, explainable recommendations, human review, and audit records in a single workflow.

The landing page introduces the problem, the proposed solution, and the system architecture. The frontend is built with React. It communicates with an API-first Spring Boot backend, which persists structured applications, decisions, review states, and audit records in PostgreSQL. Spring Security provides JWT authentication and role-based access for protected underwriter actions. Apache Tika extracts text from uploaded supporting documents. The evidence layer is designed to use PostgreSQL with pgvector when the extension is available, while retaining a text-search fallback so the workflow degrades gracefully instead of failing completely.

I will now open the live underwriting workbench. The API indicator is based on an actual backend health request rather than a static label. The decision-operations ledger also identifies the deterministic policy, authenticated reviewer role, and resilient evidence mode. Below it, the production-orchestration concept is explicitly marked as not live. Its expandable stages explain how consented streaming signals, versioned policy, advanced fraud intelligence, Bedrock-assisted explanations, and human approval could extend the working governed core. The workbench then presents the current application portfolio, inclusion-oriented impact indicators, applications requiring review, and the audit trail.

I will create a New-to-Credit application for a gig worker. The applicant is twenty-nine years old, has an annual income of four hundred and eighty thousand rupees, a mobile-usage score of eighty-five, a transaction-behaviour score of eighty, and a social-signal score of seventy.

When I submit the application, the interface visualises the controlled underwriting workflow: creditworthiness analysis, fraud-risk screening, decision calculation, explanation generation, and audit logging. This animation makes the engineering pipeline understandable to the reviewer. It does not claim that five independent autonomous agents are making a lending decision.

The eligibility outcome is generated by a deterministic and reproducible underwriting policy. For this strong alternative-data profile, the application is approved with eighty-eight percent confidence and low fraud risk. The decision card shows the contributing signals, the rationale, the confidence level, and the difference between a traditional thin-file outcome and the contextual NexCredit outcome. A traditional model may reject this applicant because a bureau score is unavailable, while NexCredit can identify positive evidence without hiding the reasoning inside an unconstrained prompt.

The next scenario demonstrates responsible decisioning. I will create an application for a nineteen-year-old student with a mobile score of twenty, a transaction score of forty-five, and a social-signal score of fifty. The low mobile score would normally trigger the rejection rule. However, the age-sensitive guardrail prevents an automatic decline and changes the result to pending human review.

This is where the underwriter becomes important. An underwriter is the human professional responsible for evaluating lending risk and confirming difficult or policy-sensitive decisions. NexCredit supports that professional; it does not remove human accountability. The protected review queue allows an authorised underwriter to inspect the case, record a reviewer note, and make the final decision. The original recommendation and the human action remain separately visible in the audit history.

I will now demonstrate the multi-modal evidence workflow. I am uploading a synthetic supporting document that contains no real customer information. The document is not merely stored as an attachment. Apache Tika extracts its text and produces a bounded preview for the reviewer. I can then search for evidence such as recurring platform settlements or alternative-data underwriting.

In a production environment, pgvector can rank document evidence by vector distance. In this local demonstration, the PostgreSQL vector extension is not active, so the service reports that condition honestly and falls back to text retrieval. This resilient fallback is deliberate engineering: an optional subsystem can be unavailable without bringing down the underwriting workflow.

The decision-rationale service operates under another important boundary. The credit outcome always comes from the deterministic policy. The explanation endpoint can work locally without a remote language model and cannot create a duplicate application or modify the original decision. A guarded language model can be enabled later to improve the wording of explanations, but it is not allowed to determine eligibility. AWS Bedrock is therefore presented as a production roadmap integration rather than falsely claimed as a live dependency in this recording.

Finally, the audit trail connects the application, automated recommendation, reasoning, timestamp, review action, and final human decision. This creates decision lineage for governance, investigation, and regulatory review. The implementation is supported by fourteen passing backend tests, two passing frontend tests, a successful production frontend build, and a validated packaging workflow.

NexCredit AI is different from a basic form-and-score demonstration because it completes the governed decision loop. It combines contextual alternative signals, fraud-risk screening, fairness guardrails, document intelligence, resilient evidence retrieval, explainable recommendations, role-protected human intervention, and auditable outcomes.

The current prototype intentionally does not claim production readiness, live bureau integrations, or autonomous AI decisioning. The next engineering phase would introduce consented real-time behavioural connectors, policy versioning, fairness monitoring, production pgvector infrastructure, an AWS Bedrock explanation adapter, streaming decision signals, and cloud observability.

NexCredit AI demonstrates how Synchrony can expand responsible credit access for New-to-Credit customers without making credit decisions less transparent or less accountable. Thank you.
